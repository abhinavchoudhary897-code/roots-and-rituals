## Exhibit 1 — Warli
**Location:** Dahanu/Palghar, Maharashtra (selected map location, not the entire cultural distribution).
**Exhibit copy:** Warli wall painting brings everyday life, seasonal activities and nature into visual storytelling. Traditionally, women prepared an earth-toned wall and applied diluted rice paste with bamboo twigs. The practice has also moved onto paper and cloth.
**Artisan:** Jivya Soma Mashe helped popularise Warli painting; the 2011 Padma award announcement lists him under the spelling Jivya Soma Mase.
**Symbol guide:** Birds, trees and animals are recurring natural subjects. Avoid assigning a universal sacred meaning to each shape without further evidence.
**Story candidate:** IGNCA documents a painting about an old man, seven daughters and a helpful crab. An original educational retelling is possible only after reviewing reuse and cultural context; no archival audio is cleared.
- W1: [IGNCA, From Wall to Walls](https://ignca.gov.in/from-wall-to-walls-the-warll-paintings/) — materials, setting, subjects and story.
- W2: [PIB, 2011 Padma Awards](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=69364) — award.
- W3: [PIB, Warli artists of Palghar](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1792227&lang=2&reg=3) — artist recognition and 2021 workshop.

## Exhibit 2 — Gond painting
**Location:** Patangarh, Madhya Pradesh (selected heritage centre).
**Exhibit copy:** Contemporary Gond painting has roots in domestic visual traditions. Jangarh Singh Shyam pioneered an influential modern approach through works on paper and canvas. Mohan Singh Shyam draws on childhood experiences of birds and animals around Patangarh.
**Artisans:** Jangarh Singh Shyam and Mohan Singh Shyam. IGNCA describes Mohan's early wall painting with his mother and the sale of paintings selected by Jangarh.
**Symbol guide:** Mohan's documented works use footprints as an image of people following a path and an expanding circle to represent intensifying wind. Attribute interpretations to these specific works rather than all Gond art.
- G1: [IGNCA, The Gond of Madhya Pradesh](https://ignca.gov.in/divisionss/janapada-sampada/tribal-art-culture/adivasi-art-culture/the-gond-of-madhya-pradesh/) — modern painting and Jangarh.
- G2: [IGNCA, Mohan Singh Shyam](https://ignca.gov.in/divisionss/janapada-sampada/tribal-art-culture/tribal-art-culture-of-madhya-pradesh-rajasthan/mohan-singh-shyam/) — individual profile and artwork interpretation.

## Exhibit 3 — Bastar Dhokra
**Location:** Bastar region, Chhattisgarh.
**Exhibit copy:** Dhokra artisans form metal objects through lost-wax casting. The Bastar district administration describes beeswax, earth, paddy husk and other mould materials, and the detailed use of wax strands for surface ornament. The process demands careful skilled work.
**Technique guide:** Model in wax, form a mould, remove wax, introduce molten metal, cool and finish. This is a simplified educational sequence, not a metalworking tutorial.
**Artisan:** Named profile and financial details held back until a full authoritative profile is checked.
- D1: [District Bastar, Handicraft](https://bastar.gov.in/en/departments/handicraft/) — regional materials and craft.
- D2: [Incredible India, Bastar Dhokra](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/chhattisgarh/bastar-dhokra) — lost-wax metal casting.

## Exhibit 4 — Sohrai: Santhal women's tradition
**Location:** Hazaribagh district, Jharkhand.
**Exhibit copy:** The Tribex archive documents Santhal women painting Sohrai motifs on homes and preparing pigments from earth. Birds and animals feature in the documented examples. Knowledge is transmitted across generations, and some women also sell their paintings.
**Artisan profile:** A community-level profile of documented Santhal women artists, not an invented named individual. Sohrai is not exclusive to the Santhal community.
**Symbol guide:** Use the documented bird motif as an example of nature-related imagery, not a claim of one universal meaning.
- S1: [Tribex, Santhal woman creating Sohrai art on a wall](https://tribex.tribal.gov.in/repository/details/OTk%3D) — motif, pigments and transmission.
- S2: [Tribex, traditional painting technique](https://tribex.tribal.gov.in/repository/details/OTY%3D) — craft and income.
- S3: [Tribex, Sohrai paintings at Sanskriti Museum](https://tribex.tribal.gov.in/repository/details/MTA0) — exhibition and sales.

## Exhibit 5 — Pithora
**Location:** Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh; Chhota Udepur is a proposed map centre, pending precise location review.
**Exhibit copy:** Pithora is a ritual painting tradition associated with Rathwa, Bhilala, Naik and Bhil communities. It is presented here as living cultural heritage, not merely decorative imagery.
**Artisan:** Paresh Rathwa, Padma Shri recipient and Pithora artist, was announced as a demonstrator at the 2023 G20 Crafts Bazaar.
**Symbol guide:** Defer detailed religious interpretation and reproduction of ritual compositions until culturally appropriate sources and permissions are confirmed.
- P1: [PIB, 8 September 2023, Tribes India pavilion](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1955624&lang=2&reg=48) — communities, artist, exhibition.
- P2: [PIB, G20 Crafts Bazaar coverage](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=1956343&lang=2&reg=48) — live demonstration.
